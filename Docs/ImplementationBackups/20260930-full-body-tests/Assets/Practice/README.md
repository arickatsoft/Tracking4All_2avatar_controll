# Solo heavy-bag practice

Choose Tracking4All or RTMPose on the home screen, then **Practice / Heavy Bag**. Remy is the local camera-controlled fighter. Practice does not start the multiplayer IP prompt or stream pose/video. **Home** restores the original fighter scale, position, cameras, lights, and arena.

## Reference presentation

`Resources/PracticeGymBackdrop.png` is a fixed gym image generated with the built-in image-generation tool from the user's gym reference. It contains no fighter or punching bag. `PracticeBackdrop` fits it to the camera without stretching. The three-dimensional avatar, rounded red bag, contact shadows, and hit particles render over the image. The practice camera stays in a fixed three-quarter rear view; it does not follow camera-tracking jitter. The fighter is normalized to 1.7 Unity metres using bone-chain lengths, rather than the changing head-to-foot span of a crouch or kick. The bag's resting distance is computed from the rig's arm length.

The bottom-left preview fits the entire upright camera frame and overlays confident body joints in green, for both tracking providers. It cannot show legs that are outside the camera image. With an upward-facing front camera, keep shoulders, hips, elbows and hands visible. Feet can remain outside the picture in assisted punch practice; unseen legs retain the authored stance. **Calibrate** resets RTMPose scale/ROI and estimates the screen-normal elevation from mobile gravity. Fine-tune height and tilt under **Tracking**. If no camera is available, the preview explicitly says so; reconnect a camera and press Calibrate to retry.

## Contact behavior

The visible bag is a 28 kg rigidbody suspended with a constrained joint. Gravity brings it back; linear/angular damping settles it. A swept sphere follows each fist/shoe tip after avatar retargeting and grounding. Only a moving, sufficiently reliable limb that reaches the bag generates an impact. Each limb must leave the bag before another hit, and tracking loss, reacquisition inside the bag, teleports, and stationary overlap do not generate hits. Real collision geometry is used; prediction never auto-completes a kick or invents a bag hit.

An accepted contact applies an impulse at its position, emits a short spark/splash burst, and plays a generated punch snap or lower kick thump. Audio is synthesized locally and preallocated for playback, with no external audio downloads. Counts in the editor's scripted simulation are test contacts, not recorded human strikes.

Design references: Unity's [continuous collision detection](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.0/Documentation/Manual/ContinuousCollisionDetection.html) and Nuitrack's [skeleton-driven Unity boxing example](https://github.com/3DiVi/nuitrack-sdk/blob/master/doc/Unity_VR_Box.md). The implementation is original; no game code/assets were copied from those projects.

Physical movement-to-display latency and phone kick quality still require a device recording; neither zero latency nor measured monocular depth is claimed. ## Assisted punch practice (26 September 2026)

Practice now starts with **PUNCH ASSIST: ON** when the humanoid guard and punch clips are available. Hold a comfortable natural stance briefly, then punch from low, middle or high hand positions. A chin-height pause is no longer required. Left/right arm observations start a reversible preparation; continuing motion commits a coordinated animation, with bounded arm IK toward the bag. The avatar maintains an authored fighting stance, with bounded existing RTMPose torso/head control layered over it, instead of copying noisy reconstructed knees and arms. This mode currently assists **punches only**. Select **BODY MIRRORING** to compare the previous full-body behavior, including tracked kicks. Home restores the original pose ownership and root-motion setting.

The detector reads RTMPose's accepted 2D observations before depth reconstruction/display prediction; the baseline reads independent normalized-landmark callbacks. Features are relative to the shoulders and torso, with confidence gates, a moving arm reference, return-phase lockout, paired raise/lower rejection, reversal cancellation, discontinuity rejection and unique observation IDs. Duplicate render updates cannot count as evidence. A single camera still cannot distinguish every foreshortened punch from a non-punch; thresholds require target-phone tuning.

One animation composer owns the final pose while assistance is on. Both original skeleton retargeters and the fallback guard defer to it. The existing floor correction runs afterward. A committed punch can complete between observations; losing tracking leads to a short recovery. Each action can score at most one actual swept contact during its strike window. Preparation, recovery, an unobserved striking arm, and repeated contact cannot create hits. The target is captured at action start so a moving bag cannot pull the arm indefinitely. This changes the earlier measured-limb-only contact contract **only in assisted punch mode**.

**RECORD TEST** starts a 75-second guided diagnostic (ten seconds to get ready, then 65 seconds of exercises) with guard/rest, slow/fast punches on each side, alternating punches, withdrawals and negative movements. It records independent raw upper-body 2D observations, confidence, detector states/reasons, timestamps, action IDs, animation phase, final local humanoid rotations/root and hit counts. JSONL files go to `Application.persistentDataPath/PunchSessions`; the complete path is logged on stop. The bounded background writer records dropped-record counts in its end marker. Recording is optional and allocates snapshots while enabled; compare performance with recording off before making latency claims.

This revision does **not encode camera video**. For visual correlation, run the phone's screen recorder alongside RECORD TEST; the visible REC time and frame ID align it with the log. RTMPose observation time is Unity camera-frame availability, not exposure time. Baseline time is callback delivery. Neither measures physical movement-to-display latency. No camera images or logs are uploaded.

Validation: **Tools → FitFight → Practice → Validate assisted punches**, report `Logs/Practice/punch-validation.txt`. Includes causal recognition at 10/30/60 Hz, negative movements, feints, duplicate observations, rearming, real bag sweep gating, one-hit action IDs, rig-length-preserving IK, resource clip references, and recorder completion. Tests are deterministic; live phone recognition and subjective animation quality still require a person in frame.

Both imported hooks now map their authored contact times to action phase 0.4. A smoothly blended elbow pole keeps the aimed elbow outward/downward; the wrist retains its authored local rotation, rather than counter-rotating against the forearm. Feet stay anchored to the guard stance, reach stays below full limb extension, and the whole-body entry/recovery blends avoid snapping into or out of assistance.

Validated on 27 September: 51 deterministic punch checks passed, as did the existing RTMPose regression/GPU inference smoke suite (its optional reference-person image was unavailable). `PunchPlayValidation.RunBatch` also runs the actual practice scene, verifies left/right observation → animation → one physical bag contact, and checks mode toggling, return home, and reentry. Reports and actual Play-mode pose captures are under `Logs/Practice/punch-play-validation.txt` and `Logs/Practice/play-punch-*.png`. Those captures use synthetic inputs; they do not demonstrate human-camera recognition accuracy. Editor-only preview scenes were discarded because their skinned meshes were stale; the retained captures come from the running scene.

## Spoken recording guide (28 September 2026)

**RECORD TEST now speaks its directions automatically**, using the phone's native text-to-speech engine in English. Turn up media volume before starting. There are ten setup seconds (including “three, two, one”), followed by the existing 65-second experiment: **75 seconds total**. Slow and fast left/right punches, guard/relax, alternating punches, withdrawals, overhead raises, stepping out, and completion each have their own spoken cue. The visible prompt uses the same schedule. You do not need to read the screen while moving.

Manual stop announces “Recording stopped.” Leaving practice, switching to body mirroring, or putting the app in the background cancels pending speech. Missed frames skip expired prompts rather than queueing old directions. An unavailable engine or missing English voice displays a voice warning while the landmark recording continues. The Unity Editor logs the spoken text; actual sound uses native engines on the phone. This change does not fix or alter punch recognition.

Android uses [TextToSpeech](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/speech/tts/TextToSpeech), including its initialization callback, media audio routing, queue flushing, shutdown, and Android 11+ TTS service visibility declaration. iPhone uses [AVSpeechSynthesizer](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avspeechsynthesizer), linked through AVFoundation. The app does not send camera or landmark data to the speech engine; it sends only the fixed instructions. Voice availability and any voice downloads are controlled by the phone's installed speech engine.

Build Android with **Tools → FitFight → Practice → Build Android spoken test**. Output: `Builds/FitFight-spoken-punch-test.apk`. Install the updated build before testing; previously installed builds cannot speak the new prompts. The prompt schedule/restart/no-backlog checks are included in **Validate assisted punches** (66 checks total). Native bridge compilation is checked separately; audible output still needs a target-device test.

## Flexible punch response revision (28 September 2026)

Current Android build: `Builds/FitFight-punch-response.apk` (**Tools → FitFight → Practice → Build Android punch response**). Session version: `punch-assist-3-outward-intent`. This supersedes the earlier spoken-test APK.

The reference follows arm chambering at different heights, including lifting a hanging arm into a punch without stopping at the chin. Extension requires opening and outward travel; lateral hooks have a separate coordinated wrist/elbow path. Preparation starts on the first qualifying observation; a second independent observation confirms animation completion, including a fast arm already held extended. A held extension cannot become a new punch origin. Return motion rearms the detector without requiring the original hand position. Both arms are checked for correlated raise/lower gestures before either can trigger. Those shared gestures intentionally do not score punches.

Readiness now reflects recognizer state and visibility. The idle hit counter no longer claims READY regardless of tracking. Spoken instructions request visible shoulders, hips and arms, explicitly allow cropped feet, and ask for punches from varied hand positions. Continuous calibrated RTMPose torso/head estimates are added to the authored pose with bounded total offsets (28° torso, 30° head), preserving lengths and planted feet. Forward bend remains the existing monocular estimate, not measured depth. Baseline Tracking4All still gets assisted punches but has no equivalent torso/head estimator for this overlay.

Validation: 150 deterministic checks, including 756 recorded observations, six visually identified outward attempts and five return windows, both confirmed negative gestures, low/side/normal starts on both sides at 10/30/60 Hz, lateral hooks, and hanging-arm chambering. Actual-scene tests cover both hands making exactly one physical bag contact, authored contact poses, ownership toggling and reentry. Reports: `Logs/Practice/punch-validation.txt`, `phone-replay-events.csv`, and `punch-play-validation.txt`. See `Docs/PunchSessions/20260928-punch-response-update.md` for measured scope and remaining limitations.

This is an improved punch controller, not a validated all-motion recognizer. Assisted kicks and squat/jump completion are not implemented. Body mirroring still requires reliable observed legs for leg contacts. Perfect recognition from all views and zero latency are not achievable with these monocular observations; the supplied phone recording had approximately 10 independent observations/second. Same-session replay is development evidence, not an independent device accuracy or latency benchmark.
